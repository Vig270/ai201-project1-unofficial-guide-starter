COURSE: CS 220 (Systems Fundamentals / C Programming Track at Binghamton University)

OVERVIEW:
CS 220 is a systems-level computer science course that introduces students to low-level programming concepts, typically using C and related systems tools. The course is known for being challenging due to its focus on memory management, pointers, and systems thinking.

PROFESSOR REVIEWS:

Thomas Bartenstein:
- Lectures are often described as overly complex and not beginner-friendly.
- Slides are considered dense and hard to follow.
- Explanation of code and concepts is sometimes insufficient.
- Some students report low engagement and difficulty understanding lecture content.
- Teaching style assumes prior knowledge that many students may not have.

Aravind Prakash:
- Known for pushing students to deeply understand concepts.
- Assignments and labs build on previous work, meaning earlier mistakes can affect later performance.
- Conceptually challenging but intellectually rigorous.
- Students may find material easy in class but significantly harder in assignments.

STUDENT EXPERIENCE SUMMARY:
- Course difficulty is moderate to high depending on prior experience with C and systems programming.
- Requires independent learning outside lectures.
- Labs and assignments are critical to understanding material.
- Success depends heavily on early preparation and consistency.

RECENT CONTEXT:
Recent semesters include Fall 2024–Fall 2026 offerings with multiple instructors including Bartenstein, Prakash, and others.
